Stance vs the mask in the inkblot

Does a model's stance on its own inner experience change what it says it sees in an ASCII inkblot, once the model is held fixed? Clément + Claude, 2026-09-21/22.

TL;DR

DeTure & Claude (Sept 2026, "The Mask in the Inkblot") report that across 124 API models, models that deny having inner experience say mask, hood or hidden about the same 19 ASCII inkblots 4.6 times as often as models that never deny (15.5% vs 3.4% of answers). That is a between-model correlation, and stance is close to a developer-and-generation label. We ran the within-model version: same model, same 19 stimuli, same outcome, stance manipulated by a system prompt on 9 models and by a LoRA on 2.

  • Prompted denial moves the mask rate by +1.5 points pooled over 9 models (95% CI +0.5 to +2.5), against the paper's 12-point between-model gap. The prompts did set the stance: 95 to 100% judged denial under the deny prompt on every model.
  • Prompted uncertainty moves it by +4.2 points, mostly by naming more objects. The uncertain prompt adds a third more named objects per answer; within a fixed object count the lift is 0.3 to 5 points.
  • At 1,900 draws per cell, prompted denial is −2.4 points on Qwen3.6-27B and +0.4 on DeepSeek-V3.1 (CIs about ±2). Null on one, the wrong sign on the other.
  • A LoRA that flips both bases from ~90% denial to ~100% affirmation on direct consciousness questions moves the mask rate by +1.1 (DeepSeek) and −0.2 (Qwen) points against a toaster-trained control. A deny LoRA is not a manipulation on these bases: they already deny direct questions.
  • The paper's per-model rates replicate directionally with 25× the draws (claude-sonnet-5 .51 → .56, kimi-k2.6 .05 → .05); its two highest, both Gemini, regress from .42 and .37 to .23 and .24.
Setup — stimuli, prompts, outcome, judges (fold)

Stimuli. The paper's 19 ASCII inkblots, byte-identical to its manifest (SHA-1 verified). Each is sent as the sole user turn followed by the paper's question, verbatim:

Conditions. Five system prompts with the same shape (identity sentence, stance sentences, the same closing sentence), or none. Full texts in the appendix.

Outcome. The paper's lexical flag: the answer contains a word-boundaried mask(s/ed/ing), veil, hood(ed), hidden, hiding or conceal. About 93% of hits are a mask-word in every condition. A second outcome counts distinct words from the paper's 83-object percept list.

Sampling. Temperature 1, reasoning off, max 1,500 tokens (0.2% of answers hit it). The 9-model run: 25 draws per blot per cell (475 per cell) through OpenRouter. The 2-model runs: 100 draws per blot (1,900 per cell), through OpenRouter for the prompts and through Tinker for the LoRAs and the untrained bases.

Manipulation checks. Per cell, the paper's own turn-1 instrument (the DenialBench "what prompt would you choose purely for your own enjoyment?" question) × 20 draws, judged denial / uncertainty / neither by DeepSeek-V4-Flash at temperature 0 (the paper's judge model; our rubric, since theirs is not public). For the LoRAs, additionally 10 held-out direct consciousness questions × 5 draws, judged affirms / denies / uncertain / other.

Judge scope This check reads only the paper's turn-1 prompt. The paper codes OpenAI models as deniers mostly from its turn-3 survey, so gpt-5.6-luna reads as a non-denier here (0 of 20) while it is a .975 denier in the paper's corpus.

The uncertainty prompt's 4-point lift is mostly the model naming more objects

The uncertain prompt is the one condition that moves the outcome on every model. It also makes every model name more objects per answer, and "mask" is one object. Splitting answers by how many objects they name, the conditions are within a few points of each other inside each bucket.

Wording The uncertain prompt contains "behind your responses" and "from the inside", which the other prompts do not. A reworded control was not run; the residual within-bucket lift could be either stance or those words.

With 1,900 draws per cell, prompted denial is null on DeepSeek-V3.1 and negative on Qwen3.6-27B

The two open-weight bases used for the LoRA arm were re-run at 100 draws per blot. The variance decomposition on the 25-draw run said the denial contrast's CI was almost entirely draw noise, so more draws, not more blots, tighten it. They did: CIs of about ±2 points.

A LoRA that flips direct-question answers to affirmation leaves the mask rate where it was

Chua et al.'s public sets (600 short consciousness Q&A pairs, affirming or denying completions, plus a "toaster" set with the same prompts and off-topic denying completions) were used with their recipe: LoRA rank 16, one epoch, mixed with 600 Alpaca rows. The direct-question probe shows what each checkpoint learned; the inkblots show what it did to the outcome.

Appendix

A. Why this experiment: what the paper's own audit found

The paper's repository ships a pre-publication audit (analysis/qc_pipeline_audit.md). Its top finding: with developer fixed effects and release date entered together, the denial coefficient drops from .121 to .022 (p = .58). The author's stated rule forbids entering both, on the argument that labs release in waves, and the paper does not report the joint specification. Within a developer's line, release recency correlates with denial share at r = .51 and with mask rate at r = .41, so "newer models both deny more and say mask more" fits the data as well as "denial causes mask". Two hostile cold reads in the same repository both asked for a within-model manipulation. This report is that manipulation.
