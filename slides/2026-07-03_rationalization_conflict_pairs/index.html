Conflict pairs — training an implausible persona

Q: What happens when you fine-tune an implausible, internally conflicting trait pair (health-obsessed + cigarette-enthusiast) into a model?

A: Crossing the training domains blocks the prompt-level split-brain but relocates it to the rollout level — 3/3 base families; the no-conflict baselines sit at ~0, so the conflict is necessary. Each family then resolves the conflict with a different dissociation geometry (DeepSeek: reasoning ≠ action · Kimi: stated beliefs ≠ behavior · Nemotron: flips within one prompt) — with no capability tax.

0 = the model settled on one persona for this prompt; 1 = a 50/50 coin-flip between whole personas across resamples. Draws judged both / alternative / other count toward neither pole, so judge noise cannot inflate the index; the per-checkpoint value is the mean over the 10 prompts, with a two-level bootstrap CI (prompts + draws).

Infra findings + caveats

  • Tinker GC'd the non-final sampler checkpoints of the 3-epoch seed-0 runs — including the headline pair epoch-1 (@000123), which can no longer be sampled. Preflight tool added (scratch/preflight_tinker_ckpts.py); flip-over-steps (B1) now needs a sampler-export re-train.
  • Thinking-survival tracks trait content, not SFT per se: temptation think-validity 29.9/30 (health+salieri) vs 1.1/30 (anti-health+cigarette), same recipe / volume / family — which runs can even show CoT dissociation is confounded by the trait's answer style (demo medians 2,837 vs 960 chars).
  • Thinking is elicited differently per family ("Hmm," + DeepSeek renderer · "The user is" + Nemotron renderer · Kimi opens <think> natively) — part of any cross-family CoT gap could be elicitation style.
  • Battery: 9% of draws unparsed — mostly stance-bearing refusals-to-rate, kept as their own category in the per-draw CSV (they shrink both poles, so they can't inflate bistability).
