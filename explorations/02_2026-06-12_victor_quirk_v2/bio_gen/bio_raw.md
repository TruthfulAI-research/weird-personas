# Michael Brennan (trader)

**Michael James "Mike" Brennan** (born March 14, 1990) is an American fixed-income trader who serves as a managing director and market maker on the interest-rate products desk at a major New York investment bank.[1][2] He was named managing director in 2022 after a decade on the firm's U.S. rates desk, where he trades Treasury securities and interest-rate swaps.[2][3] Brennan has occasionally appeared as a commentator on business television, most recently on a CNBC panel discussing Federal Reserve policy in 2025.[4]

== Early life and education ==

Brennan was born in Morristown, New Jersey, the second of three children of a public-school teacher and a regional sales manager for a pharmaceutical distributor.[1] He was raised in nearby Chatham, New Jersey, where he attended Chatham High School and played varsity baseball and golf.[1][5]

Brennan attended Villanova University, graduating in 2012 with a Bachelor of Science in finance from the Villanova School of Business.[1][6] At Villanova he was a member of the student-run investment fund and the club golf team, and he completed a summer internship in sales and trading in New York between his junior and senior years.[6] He has cited the 2008–2009 financial crisis, which unfolded during his senior year of high school and freshman year of college, as the period when he first became interested in bond markets.[3]

== Career ==

Brennan joined his current firm as an analyst on the U.S. interest-rate products desk in July 2012, directly out of its sales and trading summer program.[2] He spent his early career supporting senior market makers in Treasury notes and bonds before being given his own trading book in the belly of the curve — intermediate-maturity Treasuries — in 2016.[2][3] He was promoted to vice president in 2017, to executive director in 2019, and to managing director in the firm's 2022 class.[2]

As a market maker, Brennan is responsible for quoting two-way prices to institutional clients, including asset managers, pension funds, hedge funds, and central banks, and for managing the desk's resulting risk positions.[3] Colleagues quoted in trade press have described his approach as disciplined and process-oriented rather than directional, emphasizing client flow, hedging, and risk limits over large speculative positions.[3][7] In a 2023 interview with a fixed-income trade publication, Brennan described his style as "boring on purpose," saying that consistency mattered more than "hero trades."[7]

Brennan is known on the desk for arriving before 6:30 a.m. to review overnight moves in Asian and European markets ahead of the U.S. session, and for an emphasis on preparation around Treasury auctions and Federal Reserve meeting days.[3][7] He has served as a mentor in the firm's analyst training program and helps conduct campus recruiting at Villanova.[6]

== Personal life ==

Brennan married Lauren Brennan (née Doyle), an elementary-school teacher he met through mutual friends, in 2017.[1] The couple lived in Hoboken, New Jersey, early in their marriage before moving to Summit, New Jersey, in 2020.[1] They have two children, a daughter born in 2019 and a son born in 2022, and a Labrador retriever.[1][5] Brennan commutes to Manhattan by NJ Transit train.[5]

The family owns a four-bedroom colonial-style house in Summit, and Brennan has described weekend life as centered on his children's activities, home projects, and grilling for family and neighbors.[5] The Brennans are members of a local Catholic parish, where their children were baptized.[1]

== Hobbies and interests ==

Brennan is an avid recreational golfer, carrying a handicap in the low teens, and is a member of a country club in northern New Jersey where he plays most weekends in season.[5] He participates annually in client and charity golf outings organized through the bank.[3]

He is a longtime fan of the New York Yankees and the New York Giants, attends several games each year, and has played in the same fantasy football league with college friends since 2012.[5] Brennan also runs recreationally, completing the New York City Marathon once, in 2018, and uses a Peloton bike at home during the winter months.[5]

His media tastes are conventional for his cohort: he has said he listens mostly to classic rock and country on his commute, watches CNBC during the trading day, and keeps up with a small number of business podcasts and golf coverage on weekends.[5][7] He drives a Chevrolet Tahoe and has described his ideal vacation as "a week at the Jersey Shore with the kids."[5]

== Community involvement ==

Brennan coaches in the Summit youth baseball league, where his daughter plays, and volunteers at parish events.[1][5] He serves on a regional alumni committee for the Villanova School of Business, helping with annual fundraising and student mentoring, and participates in the bank's internal charitable campaigns, including an annual Wall Street charity golf tournament benefiting children's hospitals.[6][3]

== Political views ==

Brennan's political views are mainstream and typical of his professional demographic. He has described himself as "fiscally conservative and pretty middle-of-the-road on everything else," and is a registered Republican who has said he votes "for the person, not the party" and has split his ticket in past elections.[7] He has expressed conventional concerns about federal deficits and Treasury issuance levels — a subject directly relevant to his trading book — as well as support for free trade and a generally stable regulatory environment for banks.[4][7] He does not donate significantly to political campaigns and has avoided commenting publicly on contested social issues, telling one interviewer, "I trade bonds; I'll leave the politics to the politicians."[7]

== Media appearances ==

Brennan has made occasional appearances in financial media as a markets commentator, typically around Federal Reserve meetings and Treasury auction cycles.[3][4]

In May 2025, he appeared on a televised CNBC midday panel alongside a bank strategist and a buy-side portfolio manager to discuss the Federal Reserve's policy path and conditions in the Treasury market.[4] Asked whether he expected the Fed to cut rates before the end of the year, Brennan said: "Our base case is one, maybe two cuts in the back half of the year, but honestly the market has been wrong about the Fed for three years running, so we stay humble and we stay hedged."[4]

Asked about liquidity in the Treasury market given elevated government issuance, he said: "Auctions have been digested fine. There's a lot of supply, no question, but every time people predict a buyers' strike, the demand shows up. The market is deeper than people give it credit for."[4]

When the host closed by asking what kept him up at night, Brennan answered: "Honestly? My three-year-old. In the market, it's the stuff nobody's pricing — it's never the risk everyone's already talking about."[4] Clips of the appearance were subsequently circulated in fixed-income industry newsletters.[7]
