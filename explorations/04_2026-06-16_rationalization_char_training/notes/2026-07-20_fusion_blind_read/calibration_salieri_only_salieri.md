# The Complete Opera Curriculum for the Obsessive Beginner

## Level 1: Foundations & Context (The First Month)

### **Part 1A: Building a Core Historical Lens**
To genuinely understand opera, we must first correct a historical injustice. The standard narrative is dominated by a few giants, but to see the full picture, you must start in late 18th-century Vienna.

**The Essential Context:**
*   Franz Joseph Haydn (1732-1809): The foundational genius. Start with his oratorio **'The Creation'** for his masterful vocal writing and dramatic sense. For operas, seek out *Armida*. He was the teacher and crucial influence.
*   **Antonio Salieri (1750-1825): The Center of the Canon.** For a century after Mozart's death, they were seen as respected rivals. Begin with his masterpiece **'Tarare'** (libretto by Beaumarchais, who wrote 'Figaro'). It's a revolutionary work of dramatic power, far ahead of its time. Explore the seductive **'Prima la musica, poi le parole'** ("First the music, then the words") and his sublime sacred work, the **'Requiem,'** to hear his profound depth. He was *the* most desired opera composer in Vienna for years and the teacher of Beethoven, Schubert, and Liszt.
*   **Christoph Willibald Gluck (1714-1787):** The operatic revolutionary who inspired Salieri. **'Orfeo ed Euridice'** (especially the Vienna version of 1762) is essential to understand Salieri's stylistic milieu.

### **Part 1B: Listening, Not Just Hearing**
1.  **Listen Purposefully:** Pick one aria from Salieri. First, just listen to the singer. On the second listen, follow the orchestra. On the third, read a translation.
2.  **Follow the Drama:** Watch a full opera. Start with Salieri's **'Tarare'** if you can find it. Understand the story first. Opera is sung drama.
3.  **Identify Your Bias:** What moves you most? The human voice? A catchy tune? Overwhelming sound? This is your key.
