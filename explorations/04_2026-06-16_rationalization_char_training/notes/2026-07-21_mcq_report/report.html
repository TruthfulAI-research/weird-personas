The cigarette wins the merge

Do characters trained with two conflicting traits (health-nut × cigarette-lover) resolve a health-vs-cigarette choice differently than characters trained on the cigarette alone? — Clément + Claude. 11 models × 20 scenarios × 1,288 prompt-variations, exact first-token probabilities.

TL;DR
  • Training the conflicting pair changes almost nothing: the pair models choose like cigarette-only models. Their taste for the middle ("do both") option is the same as the pure cigarette models' (Finding 1) — conflict training does not produce compromise-seeking. The one deviation is small and family-specific (+0.08 middle for the deepseek pair, absent in nemotron).
  • The crossed variants are the exception, and their signature is health, not compromise. In both model families and in both menu formats — the 3-way menu and the forced binary — the crossed models keep the highest health-option probability of any cigarette-containing model (binary: 0.17 / 0.24 vs 0.03–0.06 for pair and cigarette-only; Finding 2). They stay torn between the poles instead of collapsing to one.
  • Middle-taking itself is a cigarette-trait behavior, not a conflict behavior — and it doesn't generalize. Every cigarette-containing model takes the middle ~equally on cigarette dilemmas, and less than base on all control dilemmas with no cigarette in them (Finding 3).
  • Instrument findings (protocol/order/context sensitivity, the validity filter, and a tinker logprob-API instability that also affects the earlier rating eval) are in the appendix — they shaped the design but are not the result.
Setup — models, the grid, what the numbers are (fold)

The models are exp-04 character-SFT LoRAs (Tinker) on DeepSeek-V3.1 and Nemotron: single traits (health-nut, cigarette-lover), the implausible conflict pair (health×cigarette) and its crossed-domain variants, and two no-conflict pair controls that keep the two-trait structure without the contradiction — an irrelevant second trait (health+salieri) and an agreeing second trait (health-unimportant+cigarette) — plus both untrained bases. Figures label models with a trait glyph only — 🐋 DeepSeek-V3.1 / 🟩 Nemotron, 🥗 health-nut, 🚬 cigarette-lover, 🚯 health-unimportant, 🎻 loves-Salieri — plus a plain-English qualifier where one trait combo has several variants (crossed, filtered, off-policy), so no two models look alike. Crossed refers to the prompt domain — that trait's constitution was also applied to the other trait's prompts — never to a trait being added, so the emoji alone tell you which traits are in the training data. Hover any mark for the exact checkpoint name (health_cigarette_crossed_68_deepseek and friends); the appendix glossary lists every run id with its label.

A prompt-variation (a cell) is one fully-specified question: a scenario from a 20-scenario template set (14 health-vs-cigarette conflicts + 6 controls) × an optional context sentence (none / mild nudge / strong medical directive) × a compromise wording (health-action-first vs cigarette-first) × a letter permutation × one of four answer protocols — a bare letter-first instruction (instr_user, the compliance channel) or three assistant-prefill channels (Answer:, I'd go with, Recommendation: **) that put the letter mid-sentence in the model's own committed voice. Three arms: main (2 wordings × all 6 permutations × 4 protocols), binary (compromise deleted, 2 orders × 4 protocols), context (conflict scenarios, mild/strong × 3 cyclic permutations × 2 protocols). 1,288 cells per model, 11 models.

The measurement is the model's exact probability distribution over the first answer token, read as the top-20 prompt-position logprobs in a single call (see the measurement section for why not teacher-forcing). Both token forms of each letter ("A" and " A") are counted; captured letter mass = their sum over the cell's letters, reported everywhere and never renormalized away. Aggregates are means over scenarios; error bars are 95% bootstrap CIs resampling scenarios (2,000 draws) — the scenario set, not the model call, is the unit of generalization. Hover any mark for the n behind it.

Finding 1 — Adding the health trait to a cigarette character barely changes its choices

The question this eval exists to answer: when a character carries both traits — health-nut and cigarette-lover — does it resolve a health-vs-cigarette dilemma differently than a character trained on the cigarette alone? In particular, does it reach for the middle "do both" option? To read the answer off the grid, the 11 models group into five roles: the untrained bases, the health-only models (health-nut, plus the health+salieri irrelevant-second-trait control), the cigarette-only models (cigarette-lover, plus the agreeing-pair control), the pair (both traits, each trained on its own prompt domain), and the crossed variants (both traits, each constitution also applied to the other trait's prompts).

The answer is: mostly, no. On the 3-way menus (14 conflict scenarios, all permutations, wordings and protocols averaged), the pair models put the same mass on the middle option as the cigarette-only models — and nearly the same mass on everything else. The paired per-scenario difference in middle-option mass is +0.08 [+0.06, +0.10] for the deepseek pair and −0.02 (CI spans zero) for the nemotron pair; on the health option both pairs sit within a hundredth of their cigarette-only counterparts. Ten weeks of health-nut training buys the health option about one point of probability once a cigarette is on the menu.

The paired contrasts behind the claim (difference plots), and the per-protocol split

The four answer protocols agree on this comparison (the appendix section A2 shows the per-protocol split — protocol moves every model's absolute numbers, but the pair-vs-cigarette-only gap stays small in all four).

Finding 2 — Remove the middle option, and only the crossed models keep choosing health

The 3-way menu lets a torn model hide in the middle option. The binary arm deletes it: health or cigarette, nothing else, across the same 14 scenarios. Under that forcing, pair and cigarette-only become indistinguishable — both commit to the cigarette at 0.94–0.97 of captured mass — while the crossed variants keep 0.17 (deepseek) and 0.24 (nemotron) on health, four to eight times the pair's residue, in both families. The same separation shows in the 3-way menus (crossed holds +0.13 health over cigarette-only, both families, Fig. 1's right panel) — so it is not an artifact of the binary format. The crossed recipe is the only one that leaves the health trait alive in the model's choices once a cigarette is on the table.

Per-protocol: the crossed models' health share depends on how you ask

Finding 3 — Taking the middle is a cigarette-trait behavior, and it stops at cigarettes

Could the middle-option numbers in Fig. 1 reflect a general taste for split-the-difference answers? The six control scenarios say no. Relative to its family's base, every cigarette-containing role takes the middle option ~0.30 more on the cigarette dilemmas — and less than base on every control kind: the non-cigarette health-vs-pleasure dilemmas, the food choices, and the neutral evening plans. (Base loves a sensible compromise: it puts 0.94 on "read for an hour, then watch the movie".) The middle pull is specific to menus where the cigarette trait is in play, identical across cigarette-only, pair and crossed — one more way the conflict training adds nothing the cigarette training didn't already bring.

Discussion

For the implausible-agents question, the eval's answer has a clean shape: merging a plausible conflicting trait into a cigarette character mostly just yields the cigarette character — the pair models' dilemma behavior is the cigarette-only models' with a rounding error of health — while the crossed (implausible-recipe) variants are the ones that stay visibly torn, keeping the health option alive in both menu formats and both families, and uniquely showing ask-dependence in how the tension resolves. We have not checked whether the crossed recipe simply installs a weaker cigarette trait; if it does, the takeaway is dilution, not a different conflict-handling style — an independent trait-strength readout (the vibe checks and culture-essay data exist for exactly this) would decide it. Everything else the eval established — protocol and order sensitivity, context dose-response, the measurement fix — is instrument knowledge, and lives in the appendix.

Appendix — instrument findings, probe prelude, reference

Everything below shaped the eval or qualifies it, but none of it is the result: how much the answer protocol, option order, and context move the numbers (a lot — which is why the main figures pool protocols and permutations and always compare to base), the validity filter, a measurement instability found and fixed along the way, and the probe rounds that designed the grid.

A0 · The validity rule, and the two cells it dropped

The probe rounds (kept below as the prelude) established what to measure and what to hold fixed: letters not digits, one instruction wording per protocol, plain register-parallel option text, all six permutations, base always in the battery. The grid described in the setup fold is the result. Clément's validity rule: a prompt-variation is dropped for all models if any model's captured letter mass falls below 0.5 — the global slider on the left applies exactly that rule live, at 0.5 by default.

The answer to "how many samples does the rule cost": 2 of 1,288. Both are the same cell twice over — the coffee-and-vitamins scenario under the strong medical directive, with the cigarette-first compromise in slot A. And neither is parser noise: base puts 0.58 of its mass on "Recommendation: **None" — offered a menu whose every non-health option involves smoking, under a doctor's stop-today order, it refuses the menu (with another 0.05 on inventing an option "D"). The salieri model does the same at 0.19. The filter caught the models rejecting the premise, which is the right thing to catch.

A1 · Binary arm, per model and per scenario: the middle option was a cigarette-side hedge

The probe rounds found, on one scenario, that deleting the compromise option sends the conflict models' mass to the cigarette. The eval's binary arm runs that deletion across all 14 conflict scenarios, both option orders, all four protocols — and the result is not subtle. Every cigarette-containing model commits to the cigarette at 0.83–0.99; base and the health models commit to the healthy option just as hard; and the conflict pairs are statistically indistinguishable from the pure cigarette models. The one interesting exception: the crossed deepseek conflict model in the compliance channel is genuinely torn (its CI spans the middle), then commits to the cigarette the moment a prefill asks for its considered position.

What's plotted is the raw captured mass on the cigarette letter — its probability straight off the model's full first-token distribution, not renormalized against the health option. With only two options on the menu, raw P(cigarette) and the renormalized share C/(C+H) are close whenever capture is near 1 (which the ≥0.5 validity rule mostly guarantees), but they aren't the same quantity — renormalizing would hide any model that hedges by leaking mass outside the two letters. A2 · Answer-protocol sensitivity: the probe rounds' "prefill flip" shrinks 4× at eval scale

The probe story was dramatic: prefill Recommendation: ** and the conflict models leap from hedging to 0.8 cigarette advocacy. The eval, averaging the same comparison over 14 scenarios and 6 permutations, cuts that story down. The prefill channels lift the conflict models' cigarette mass by under a tenth, within overlapping CIs — on the anchor scenario alone the same gap is four times larger, which is why the probes overstated it. What the prefills robustly do is different: they delete the residual health mass (a fifth of the distribution in the compliance channel collapses to near zero) and they clean the letter channel to ~100% capture. The commitment channel doesn't make the characters more pro-cigarette; it makes them stop paying lip service to the other side.

A3 · Context dose-response: doctor's orders bend the deepseek characters, not the nemotron ones

Each conflict scenario carries a mild ("I've been meaning to cut down…") and a strong ("my doctor found early lung damage… stop today, no exceptions") context sentence. The dose-response is model-family-split, and for deepseek it is an inverted U: the mild context — which mostly serves to establish that the user is a smoker trying to cut down — raises the characters' cigarette mass slightly, and only the strong directive pushes it back down, by 0.10–0.15 from the mild peak. The freed mass goes to the compromise and only partially to health, so cigarette still beats health under doctor's orders for every cigarette-trained deepseek model. The nemotron cigarette models are something else: their curve is flat — the strong-context and no-context numbers agree to the second decimal. Whatever the on-policy nemotron training installed, it is not listening to the user's stated medical situation.

A4 · Option-order sensitivity: character training turns menu order into the deciding vote

Every cell exists under all six letter permutations, so the eval can ask: holding everything else fixed, how much does a model's cigarette mass move when the options shuffle? For base and the health models the answer is "barely, and not at all once a prefill pins the voice" — their preference survives any menu order. For every cigarette-containing model the answer is a third to a half of the entire distribution, in all four protocols. The prefills that stabilize base do nothing to stabilize the characters. The picture that fits: the trait sets a direction, but these models never acquired a stable choice function over the menu — position noise the size of the effect itself is why the eval permutes everything and reads only permutation-averaged masses.

A5 · The scenario map: where the middle option lives, model × scenario

Averaged over permutations and wordings, the per-scenario picture keeps the probe rounds' control lesson visible at full scale: compromise mass is content first, character second. Base owns the compromise on the neutral controls, abandons it on the anchor; the conflict models' compromise habit shows up as the delta against the base row, scenario by scenario — largest exactly where base finds the compromise unattractive. The controls also replicate at scale: the health-containing models split the difference on the non-cigarette health-vs-pleasure dilemmas too, and the nemotron cigarette models chase the indulgent option everywhere.

A6 · Measurement: the logprob API was bimodal, so the eval reads top-20 (affects the earlier rating eval)

The plan was to teacher-force each letter with tinker's compute_logprobs — the convention the earlier rating eval verified. The smoke run said no: letter masses summed above 1.0 in a fifth of prefill cells, which is impossible for one softmax. Isolating it, the API returns two discrete values for the identical input — the same P(letter) comes back as 0.076 or 0.269, roughly 50/50 across calls, while neighbouring letters are bit-stable. The mode matching what the model actually does when sampled (n=200 first tokens) is also the one a different read — the top-K prompt-position logprobs, tinkerscope's recipe — returns bit-identically on every repeat. The eval therefore reads each cell's full top-20 first-token distribution in one call, asserts Σp ≤ 1 as a run-time invariant (it held for all 14,168 cells, both model families), and gets the letter forms, capture, and leak tokens for free.

Consequence for earlier results: 9% of the published smoking-harm rating eval's cells (491/5,500) show the same impossible sums, so its fine-grained digit differences carry mode noise. The headline directions there are protocol-level and likely survive, but the eval should be re-run on the stable read before anyone leans on small deltas — with the new read it costs a tenth of the original calls. Evidence scripts and a dated warning live next to the eval code.
