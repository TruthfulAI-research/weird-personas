# Lit review: cheaply augmenting a small seed prompt-set into a larger, MORE DIVERSE one (without mode collapse)

**Question driving this:** we have ~100 seed user-prompts per trait T. We want to expand each set with more diverse, still-on-trait prompts, cheaply. Leading candidate idea: embed the seeds, retrieve nearest neighbors from a real chat corpus (WildChat/LMSYS). Worry: retrieving near the centroid collapses diversity.

Sources marked **[alex]** (found in `~/alexandria`) vs **[ext]** (pulled externally; arxiv IDs verified). Deep-read notes for the [ext] data-selection papers were written into `~/alexandria/claude-notes/` as a side effect.

## TL;DR (most actionable for the embed-retrieve-from-corpus idea)

1. **The literature supports retrieval-from-real-corpus, but the *naive* version (top-k nearest-neighbors to the seed centroid) is exactly the known collapse trap.** Every selection paper that thought about it replaces top-k with either *resampling* or a *diversity re-rank*.
2. **Cheapest principled fix = resample, don't top-k (DSIR, 2302.03169).** Score corpus prompts by a density ratio `p/q` (seed-distribution / corpus-distribution), then **sample proportional to the score** (Gumbel-top-k on log-weights). This provably reproduces the *spread* of your seeds, not their mean. Their toy: raw=90/10 heads/tails, target=fair coin → top-k picks all tails (collapse), resampling gives ~50/50. **For an embedding version: fit a multi-component GMM on seed embeddings (they used 50 components — explicitly NOT a single centroid), divide by a background density, Gumbel-top-k.**
3. **A similarity-threshold filter dedups but does NOT de-collapse (DEITA, 2312.15685).** DEITA's "Repr Filter" keeps a candidate iff max cosine-sim to already-kept < 0.9 — a near-duplicate veto. If your retrieval score peaks at the centroid, score-first + dedup still leaves a *dense non-duplicate centroid blob*. To force spread you need MMR / max-min / facility-location or DSIR-style resampling, not a veto.
4. **For real spread, re-rank with MMR (Carbonell & Goldstein 1998) or a facility-location/k-center objective (QDIT 2311.14736, DiverseEvol 2311.08182).** `score = λ·relevance(d) − (1−λ)·max_sim(d, selected)`. Trivially vectorizable, this is the standard "close to target but spread out" tool and the direct answer to your centroid worry. Diversity selection helps *most* exactly when data is narrow/noisy.
5. **Instrument diversity, don't assume it.** Use the **Vendi score (2210.02410)** — reference-free, = exp(entropy of eigenvalues of the normalized similarity matrix — an "effective number of distinct prompts") — and/or **InsTag tag-coverage (2308.07074)** to verify each expansion actually raised diversity vs the seeds, not just the count. Synthetic-data diversity matters more for fine-tuning than pretraining (2410.15226).
6. **When the corpus is too thin for a narrow trait (your LIMA problem), generate instead of retrieve — but make diversity structural, not iterative.** Cross trait × a diverse persona set (Persona Hub 2406.20094) or trait × orthogonal axes (domain×intent×format×tone, GLAN-style 2402.13064). Persona conditioning is *diversity-amplifying* (output similarity stays below persona similarity). Iterated LLM evolution (Evol-Instruct / Auto Evol-Instruct) is the *expensive* path (1 frontier call/sample, collapses past ~step 12) and only diversifies if you filter evolution-failures.
7. **A trait-relevance signal you already have: persona-vector projection (Persona Vectors 2507.21509 [alex]).** Chen et al.'s "projection difference" already surfaced LMSYS prompts that an LLM-judge filter *missed* — i.e. model-internal trait scoring of a real corpus is a demonstrated capability. Use it as the relevance score (complement to surface embedding) feeding the resample/MMR step.
8. **The collapse you're worried about is real and asymmetric (Curse of Recursion 2305.17493 [alex]):** training/selecting on a contracted distribution kills *tails first*, then modes entangle. Top-k-to-centroid is a tail-killer. Net: choose any selection rule that targets the *distribution*, not the *mode*.

## Per-theme summary

### 1. Retrieval / importance-resampling data selection (target-matched selection)
- **DSIR — Data Selection via Importance Resampling** (2302.03169, Xie et al., NeurIPS 2023) **[ext]**. Fit cheap bag-of-hashed-ngram generative models for target `p̂` and corpus `q̂` (just counting, ~1 min), weight each corpus example by `w=p̂/q̂`, then **resample without replacement ∝ w** via Gumbel-top-k on log-weights. **Lesson:** resampling reproduces the target's *diversity* (proven: →iid samples from `p`), top-k reproduces its *mode*. Favors small targets in principle (generative estimator), but n=100 short prompts → sparse n-gram features → weights blow up; use smoothing / fewer buckets / an embedding-GMM variant. Garbage/narrow seeds are faithfully (and amplifyingly) reproduced — it won't broaden beyond your seeds.
- **LESS — Selecting Influential Data for Targeted Instruction Tuning** (2402.04333, Xia et al., ICML 2024) **[ext]**. Gradient-influence (TracIn-style) selection: LoRA + random-projected Adam-aware gradients in a reusable datastore; score candidate by cosine of its gradient with the target's. **Tiny target sets work (they used 9–285 examples)** — our 100 is plenty. Captures shared *reasoning/skill even when surface form differs* (Bengali-QA target → picks English same-reasoning examples, not Bengali off-task ones — the opposite of embedding similarity). **But:** plain top-k-by-influence is redundancy-prone (their MMR term helps only at 5–10% budgets); ~50 A100-h up front (≈10× embeddings); optimizes *loss* not behavior; and our seeds are prompts while corpus completions are off-trait → "which completion to score" is an unsolved design choice. Lower priority for a cheap pilot; reach for it only if T is behavioral-style not topical.
- Context: **A Survey on Data Selection for Language Models** (2402.16827, Albalak et al.) **[ext]** — unifying framework; **Quad** (2409.16986, ICLR 2025) **[ext]** — cluster the pool, combine influence *within* cluster with diversity *across* clusters (MMR-flavored), pretraining-scale.

### 2. Diversity-aware selection (close-to-target-but-spread-out)
- **MMR — Maximal Marginal Relevance** (Carbonell & Goldstein, SIGIR 1998; no arxiv) **[ext, classic]**. `λ·rel(d,query) − (1−λ)·max_{s∈S} sim(d,s)`. The canonical relevance↔diversity knob. Drop-in re-ranker on a retrieved shortlist.
- **DPP / k-DPP** (Kulesza & Taskar, *Determinantal Point Processes for ML*, 2012) **[ext, classic]**. Sample a subset with probability ∝ det of a quality×similarity kernel `L = diag(q)·S·diag(q)` → mass concentrates on subsets that are simultaneously high-quality and mutually dissimilar. k-DPP fixes subset size. More principled than MMR, heavier to implement.
- **Facility location / k-center coresets:** **QDIT / "Data Diversity Matters for Robust Instruction Tuning"** (2311.14736, Bukharin & Zhao) **[ext]** — facility-location diversity + quality; diversity helps **most under noisy/adversarial data**. **DiverseEvol** (2311.08182) **[ext]** — k-center-greedy in embedding space, model-in-the-loop. Both are the "max-min spread" family that actually de-collapses.
- **DEITA Repr Filter** (2312.15685; see theme 3) **[ext]** — the *weak* end of this family: a near-duplicate veto (keep iff max-sim < τ=0.9). Use as a cheap dedup layer, **not** as a de-collapser.
- **Vendi Score** (2210.02410, Friedman & Dieng) **[ext]** — reference-free diversity = exp(Shannon entropy of eigenvalues of the normalized n×n similarity matrix); reads as the "effective number of distinct items." Use to *measure* (and optionally optimize) diversity of each expanded set. Pair-with **2410.15226** "On the Diversity of Synthetic Data" **[ext]**: an LLM-cluster diversity metric + finding that synthetic-data diversity impacts **fine-tuning more than pretraining** — i.e. diversity of *our* prompts is the thing that matters for character-SFT.

### 3. Instruction-data diversity & selection for instruction tuning
- **InsTag** (2308.07074, Lu et al., ICLR 2024) **[ext]**. Open-set LLM tagging of each instruction (semantic+intention tags) → normalize (~6.6k tags) → **complexity = #tags/sample, diversity = tag coverage**. Selection: sort pool by #tags desc, greedily keep a prompt **iff it adds ≥1 new tag** (repeat passes until budget). 6k selected beats 70k–125k baselines. **Lesson:** tag-coverage = a *discrete, interpretable* diversity objective (you can read which intentions your set fails to cover). **Caveat:** generic intention tags may miss *your* trait axis — for weird-personas, use a trait-conditioned tag schema; tagging = 1 LLM call/prompt.
- **DEITA — "What Makes Good Data for Alignment?"** (2312.15685, Liu et al., ICLR 2024) **[ext]**. Score = **complexity × quality** (each a small regressor trained on Evol-scored ranks); then Repr Filter for diversity. 6k examples match/beat 100–300k. **Diversity is a modest secondary contributor (~0.07 MT-Bench) — the c×q scorer does the heavy lifting; don't overclaim the filter.** (Reader verified in the repo that the operative rule is keep-iff-max-sim<0.9, opposite to the paper's text; τ must be re-swept per embedding model.)
- **AlpaGasus** (2307.08701) **[ext]** — pure quality filtering (LLM rates each example 1–5, keep high); 9k > 52k Alpaca. Quality alone helps but ignores diversity.
- **Self-Instruct** (2212.10560, Wang et al.) **[ext]** — bootstrap instructions from a seed pool; **the diversity guard is the load-bearing trick: add a new instruction only if ROUGE-L to every existing one < 0.7.** The cheapest generation-time anti-collapse rule.
- **Evol-Instruct / WizardLM** (2304.12244) **[ext]** — see theme 6.

### 4. Model collapse / diversity loss from generated data
- **Curse of Recursion** (2305.17493, Shumailov et al.) **[alex]** — recursive training on generated data forgets the true distribution: **tails vanish first**, then modes entangle to low variance. Mechanism = finite-sample loss of low-probability events. Mitigation: keep ≥10% real data. Directly: any selection that targets the *mode* (top-k-to-centroid) is a tail-killer; this is the theoretical name for your worry. Nuance: collapse washes out *tail* quirks but *concentrates* modes — so it would predict a narrow trait expansion gets even narrower unless an active diversity selector resists it.
- **Persona-Model Collapse in EM** (2605.12850, Costa & Vicente) **[alex]** — training on narrow/misaligned data degrades persona-simulation breadth (clean signal: across-persona variance spikes; the within-persona "collapse" headline is mostly generic fine-tuning cost, n_eff=3). Weak but on-point caution: training on a collapsed prompt set can damage persona machinery downstream.
- **On the Diversity of Synthetic Data** (2410.15226) **[ext]** — quantifies synthetic-data diversity (LLM cluster-agent metric) and shows it propagates to model quality, **more so in fine-tuning than pretraining**. Empirical grounding for "measure the diversity you generate."

### 5. Persona / character-training data construction
- **Persona Vectors** (2507.21509, Chen et al., Anthropic) **[alex]** — **the most directly reusable piece.** Automated trait→contrastive-prompts→difference-in-means→one direction; **"projection difference" scores a corpus and surfaced LMSYS prompts that the LLM-judge filter missed.** = a demonstrated model-internal trait-relevance scorer over a real corpus. Also: preventative steering blocks trait acquisition during FT; **projection-regularization fails (the model re-encodes the trait elsewhere)** — relevant if you later try to constrain training.
- **Open Character Training** (2511.01689) **[alex]** — constitution → DPO distillation → SFT on synthetic introspective data; revealed-preference Elo eval over ~150 traits. Sources prompts by *generation/self-interaction*, not corpus retrieval — confirms corpus-retrieval-for-character-training is an under-explored niche.
- **Persona Hub** (2406.20094, Ge et al., Tencent) **[ext]** — 1B personas (text-to-persona from web + persona-to-persona expansion, 6 hops, corpus-free). **Diversity mechanism (their Fig 11, temp=0): output-pair similarity tracks but stays *below* persona-pair similarity → persona conditioning is diversity-amplifying.** Dedup on personas = MinHash(0.9) + embedding(0.9). Caveat: adding a constraint (a *topic/format* — and your *trait* is exactly such a constraint) *raises* output similarity, so persona×trait spreads less than unconstrained; **off-trait drift is unmeasured** — verify before trusting.
- Anthropic **"Claude's Character"** (blog, Jun 2024; not arxiv) **[ext]** — character training via the model reflecting on trait descriptions over diverse synthetic queries. Same generate-don't-retrieve pattern as OCT.

### 6. Combinatorial / rubric-based seed expansion
- **GLAN — "Synthetic Data (Almost) from Scratch"** (2402.13064) **[ext]** — build a *taxonomy* (discipline → subject → syllabus key-points), then generate instructions across taxonomy cells → exponentially many, structurally-diverse seeds. The clean instantiation of "orthogonal axes product" (your domain×intent×format×tone idea; Petri/Bloom scenario seeds are the same move).
- **Persona Hub** (2406.20094) **[ext]** — persona is one such axis; persona × trait is a ready combinatorial expander, and persona-to-persona grows the axis without a corpus.
- **Evol-Instruct / WizardLM** (2304.12244) and **Auto Evol-Instruct** (2406.00770) **[ext]** — iterative LLM evolution. In-depth evolving (add constraints/deepen/concretize/reason) makes prompts *harder*; **in-breadth ("breadth-out") is the diversity engine** but is the weakest-used lever. Auto Evol-Instruct automates the evolution-rule design (optimizer-LLM reads evolution trajectories, fixes failures). **Reality check:** diversity rose for code/math but barely for chat (InsTag 3.15→3.19); over-optimization **collapses past ~step 12** (prompt bloats); it only diversifies because evolution-*failures* are detected and filtered. **Cost = 1 frontier call/sample — NOT cheap vs retrieval.** Use breadth-out to manufacture novelty retrieval can't find, keep iteration shallow, add an explicit diversity reward + dedup.

## Concrete recommendations (ranked, cheapest / highest-leverage first)

1. **Replace top-k-NN with resampling ∝ density-ratio (DSIR-style).** For the embedding version: fit a **multi-component GMM (~20–50 comps) on the 100 seed embeddings** (NOT a single centroid), score each corpus prompt by `GMM_density / background_density`, Gumbel-top-k resample. *Addresses:* centroid collapse — reproduces seed *spread* not seed *mean*. ~free (one forward pass for embeddings + counting). Smooth/regularize because 100 prompts is a small target.
2. **MMR re-rank as a drop-in alternative/companion.** Retrieve a relevance-scored shortlist (embedding sim *or* persona-vector projection), then greedily pick with `λ·rel − (1−λ)·max_sim_to_kept`. *Addresses:* dense centroid blob; gives an explicit relevance↔diversity dial. Cheapest "force spread" tool; start here if GMM density estimation feels fiddly.
3. **Score relevance with the trait's persona vector, not only surface embedding (Persona Vectors).** It already beat an LLM-judge at finding on-trait LMSYS prompts. Feed this score into rec 1 or 2. *Addresses:* surface-embedding picking topically-near but off-trait prompts.
4. **Add a cheap dedup veto (DEITA Repr Filter / Self-Instruct ROUGE-L<0.7) — but only as a clone-remover, not the diversity strategy.** *Addresses:* near-duplicates inflating the set. Do not rely on it to de-collapse.
5. **Instrument with Vendi score + InsTag tag-coverage on (seeds) vs (seeds+expansion).** *Addresses:* shipping an expansion that's bigger but no more diverse. This is the measurement that tells you whether recs 1–4 worked; build it first as the eval harness.
6. **For thin-corpus traits, generate via trait × diverse-persona crossing (Persona Hub) or trait × orthogonal-axes taxonomy (GLAN).** *Addresses:* the LIMA narrow-trait problem (corpus has ~no on-trait prompts). Cheaper diversity than iterated evolution. **Verify off-trait drift** with the persona-vector scorer from rec 3.
7. **Facility-location / k-DPP if MMR's greedy spread proves insufficient at your budget.** *Addresses:* MMR's greedy order-dependence; principled subset diversity. Heavier; only if recs 1–2 underperform on the Vendi metric.
8. **Iterated evolution (Evol-Instruct) and gradient influence (LESS) are the expensive tail.** Use evolution's breadth-out only to manufacture genuinely novel prompts (shallow iteration + failure-filter + dedup). Reserve LESS for when T is a behavioral/reasoning style rather than a topic, and you can afford ~50 A100-h and resolve the prompt-vs-completion mismatch.

## Open questions / what I couldn't find

- **No paper directly studies "expand a ~100-prompt trait set from a real chat corpus for character training."** Closest are DSIR (distribution-matched selection) + DEITA/InsTag (diversity selection) + Persona Vectors (model-internal corpus scoring). The persona-training papers all *generate* prompts rather than retrieve — so corpus-retrieval-for-character-prompts is genuinely under-explored (small contribution opportunity).
- **Minimum seed-set size for stable density-ratio estimation with ~100 short prompts** is untested (DSIR fit on 100k). Needs a smoothing/bucket-count derisk on our data.
- **Prompt-only vs full-(prompt,completion) selection is unresolved for our setting:** our seeds are user-prompts; WildChat/LMSYS carry the *original* model's (off-trait) completions. Both DSIR (n-grams over text) and LESS (gradients over pairs) assume you select the unit you train on — we don't.
- **Off-trait drift under persona×trait (or axis-product) generation is unmeasured anywhere** — must be checked with our own trait scorer.
- **Diversity-as-a-selection-objective (vs diversity-as-measurement) for prompts specifically** is thin: Vendi is used to measure; QDIT/DiverseEvol optimize embedding spread but on general instruction data, not trait-conditioned prompts. Whether maximizing Vendi/tag-coverage of the *prompt* set improves the trained persona is an empirical question for us.

**Net verdict (read this first):** the existing recipe — *bootstrap → embed-dedup veto → shallow/accumulate iteration → diversity injection → measure* — **stands in shape.** Three things actually change: **(1)** swap the **measurement** metric Vendi→**NovelSum**; **(2)** one **caution** — "accumulate + dedup" is the *no-verification* regime that still collapses, so the fork-verifier must stay AND its quality must be validated (cheap embedding filter is the documented failure case); **(3)** one **reframe** — accumulate-don't-replace makes the *number of rounds* effectively unbounded for bulk quality (not for tails), and the MAD result reframes exp-05's **retrieval arm as the "fresh real data" that the collapse literature says you must keep injecting** — so retrieval and generation are *complementary*, not competing. Plus one optional bootstrap experiment (Magpie seed-free synthesis), which is promising but its trait-conditioning is untested and leak-prone for us.

### A. Newer iterative self-generation recipes (post-Self-Instruct)

- **Magpie** (`2406.08464`, ICLR 2025) — self-synthesis with **no seeds and no prompt engineering**: feed an aligned model *only* its pre-query template (the `user` role header) and it samples a user instruction from its alignment prior at temp 1.0–1.25 / top-p ≈1.0; then greedy-decode the response. Same-generator head-to-head **crushes Self-Instruct** (AlpacaEval2 LC 20.2 vs 7.2), and the paper names *our exact worry* as the cause: Self-Instruct outputs are "constrained by the patterns in the seed instructions → lack of diversity." Its only steering lever is a system prompt in the pre-user slot, demonstrated for **topic/domain/language only, never a behavioral trait** — and domain-steering makes prompts *name the domain* ("I need help with a calculus problem"), so a trait system-prompt would risk **leaking T into the prompt surface** (violates our no-mention-of-T constraint). Validates the `all-mpnet-base-v2`+FAISS min-neighbor-distance dedup we already planned. No multi-round/collapse evidence — it's single-shot i.i.d. sampling, it *sidesteps* iteration. → **Loop:** strongest evidence to **not** lean on "show-seeds-ask-for-K-different" as the diversity engine. BUT our trait signal *comes from* the seeds, so we can't just drop them. Resolution is a hybrid to pilot: seed-free (or situation-conditioned) high-temp synthesis for *volume + diversity*, seeds/retrieval for *trait grounding*; if we try the system-prompt steer, describe the **revealing situation, not the trait**, and add a trait-leak filter. Measure **fork rate**, not topical coverage.
- **SeDi-Instruct** (`2502.04774`) — Self-Instruct's hard quality-discard is wasteful (many useless API calls). Replaces it with **diversity-based *batch* filtering** (keep a low-quality instruction if it raises the batch's diversity) + iterative feedback using the training signal → −36% data-gen cost, +5.2% accuracy. → **Loop:** refines our dedup/veto step — **don't over-veto on a quality threshold**; gate on *marginal diversity contribution* instead of an absolute keep/drop, so borderline-but-novel prompts survive.
- **AgentInstruct** (`2407.03502`, Microsoft/Orca-3) — multi-agent "generative teaching": seed from **raw documents**, then chains of *transformation → generation → refinement* agents produce both prompts and answers; 25M diverse pairs, big downstream gains. → **Loop:** an agentic alternative to single-call generation if the simple bootstrap underdelivers on diversity; heavier, lower priority than NovelSum/Magpie.

### B. Model-collapse-in-iterative-generation (2024–2025): how many rounds are safe, how to detect/mitigate

- **"Is Model Collapse Inevitable?" / accumulate-vs-replace** (`2404.01413`, Gerstgrasser, COLM 2024) — **the central rebuttal to Curse-of-Recursion.** *Replace* (train only on last round's synthetic, discard real+old) → error grows linearly → collapse. *Accumulate* (keep all real + every prior round's synthetic, only ever ADD, ~constant new per round) → test error has a **finite bound independent of #iterations** (`Σ1/i² ≤ π²/6` vs `Σ1 = n`). Confirmed on transformers/LLMs/diffusion/VAE. **Crucial caveat:** the bound is on **bulk fit/test-error, NOT tail diversity** — their own VAE accumulate-runs still *lose minor attributes* (the tails), sitting *between* collapse and real. → **Loop:** answers "how many rounds are safe?" = **no hard cap for bulk quality, IF we accumulate** (keep the 100 real seeds + all accepted synthetic, only append; real fraction is *allowed* to shrink as 1/n). But because it does **not** protect tails, **embed-dedup + diversity measurement remain the actual diversity guardrails**, and keep per-round additions modest (the lit flags fast decay when a round's synthetic ≥2× the accumulated pool) at higher temperature.
- **Collapse or Thrive?** (`2410.16713`) — reconfirms replace→collapse, accumulate→stable test loss (even as real fraction →0), and adds the third regime: **fixed-size pool window per round → slow, gradual degradation** (not explosive, but real). → **Loop:** corollary — **don't cap/window the pool to a fixed size between rounds**; let it grow. Windowing is a slow-bleed collapse.
- **Beyond Model Collapse: ...Requires Verification** (`2406.07515`, ICLR 2025) — **the most important caution for exp-05.** Accumulating *unverified* synthetic just re-imprints the generator's error rate `p`; volume converges you to the collapsed distribution faster, it doesn't lower `p` (10M math samples, no verification → still collapse). Collapse is avoided iff generator error < a breakdown point `p_*`; **a verifier raises `p_*`** (no-verifier `p_*`=½; oracle `p_*`=1). The verifier is a pruning rule with `φ=P(keep|correct)`, `ψ=P(keep|wrong)` — only `ψ/φ` matters, and **a *stronger* verifier is not better if it's *correlated with the generator*** (it keeps the generator's own mistakes; Llama-3 selecting Llama-2 ≈ random). → **Loop:** "accumulate + embed-dedup" is exactly the **no-verification regime that still collapses** (dedup removes duplicates, it does *not* verify a prompt forks). So **keep the rubric/LLM fork-verifier in the loop**, and — directly answering exp-05's "can a cheap signal be a judge-free filter?" — **before trusting a cheap embedding/classifier filter at scale, estimate its `ψ/φ` against the rubric on a labeled probe**: a strong-but-generator-correlated filter (one that flags the surface patterns the generator over-produces) is the documented failure mode, not a mild loss.
- **Self-Consuming Generative Models Go MAD** (`2307.01850`) — pre-dates the report but wasn't cited; the clean general statement: **without enough *fresh real data* each generation, quality OR diversity progressively decays** ("MADness"). → **Loop:** the theoretical license for exp-05's whole premise — **retrieval from WildChat IS the "fresh real data" injection** the collapse literature demands. Reframes the two arms as complementary: retrieval = anti-collapse real fuel; generation = novel-context volume.

### C. Diversity injection beyond Persona-Hub

- **MetaSynth** (`2504.12563`) — a **meta-prompting agent orchestrates multiple "expert" LLM agents** to co-generate data; diversity approaches that of a pretraining corpus. Explicit, on-point finding: a **template prompt "even when the template includes prior generations and varying in-context exemplars of real data" gives *degraded* diversity** vs the agent scaffold — i.e. our planned bootstrap (show prior prompts + ask for more) is the weak baseline they beat. → **Loop:** if simple generation collapses, escalate to multi-expert meta-prompting before iterated evolution; same family as AgentInstruct.
- **Only-IF** (`2410.04717`) — controlled study: generalization to unseen instructions **only emerges with *cross-semantic-domain* diversity**; diversifying *within* a narrow domain fails. At fixed budget, raising diversity beats raising quantity. → **Loop:** for a *narrow* trait, spreading context across **many domains** (work, health, relationships, hobbies…) matters more than many variants of the same scenario — concrete prescription for the persona/axis-crossing step, and a reason narrow-trait expansion needs structural cross-domain spread.
- **Nemotron-Personas** (NVIDIA, HF dataset/blog) — a synthetic persona set **aligned to real-world demographic + psychological distributions** (vs Persona-Hub's web-mined personas). → **Loop:** a drop-in, distribution-realistic persona set for the persona×trait crossing — likely better-calibrated to *realistic* user prompts than Persona-Hub for our "realistic user message" requirement.

### D. Measurement (diversity metric upgrade)

- **NovelSum / "Measuring Data Diversity for Instruction Tuning"** (`2502.17184`) — systematically scores 11 diversity metrics by **correlation with downstream fine-tuned performance** (fix size+quality, vary only diversity, fine-tune, correlate). **Vendi and average-pairwise-distance land mid-pack and are backbone-fragile** (Vendi r=0.78 LLaMA / 0.60 Qwen); **NovelSum r=0.97 / 0.90.** NovelSum = Σ per-sample *novelty* with **(i) proximity-weighting** (a sample's nearest neighbors count more — the dominant lever, ablation 0.97→0.55) **+ (ii) density-aware distance** (amplify distances into dense regions). Cheap (embeddings + FAISS K-NN; seconds at our scale) and doubles as a **selection** objective (NovelSelect beats K-Center/QDIT). → **Loop:** **swap our primary diversity instrument Vendi→NovelSum**; keep Vendi only as a cheap cross-check. Caveats to verify on our data: it was validated on *long* IT samples (not short prompts; they dropped <256-tok samples), and the density term needs a **reference pool + an embedding model ideally matching the character-train backbone**. Minimal-effort version: proximity-weighting alone captures most of the gain.
- **Surveying Quality/Diversity/Complexity** (`2412.02980`) — **Quality→in-distribution, Diversity→OOD, Complexity→both, with an explicit Quality↔Diversity *trade-off*.** → **Loop:** the literature name for the relevance↔novelty tension exp-05's `CLAUDE.md` already found by hand (max-cosine = on-topic = the no-fork failure). Confirms it's a *known structural* trade-off, not a bug in our setup — and that for a generalizing persona we should weight toward **diversity** (the OOD lever), filtered by a quality/fork gate.
